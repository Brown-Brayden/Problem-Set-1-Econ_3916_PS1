# Econ 3916 PS1: The Cost of Living Brief

How much did the cost of living rise from 2019 to 2024 for one specific household,
and how does that compare with official CPI?

**Notebook:** `Econ 3916 PS1.ipynb` (runs in Google Colab)

## The household
Jordan, 27, a single dental hygienist in Columbus, Ohio, earning about $52,000 a year.
Rents a one-bedroom apartment, drives a financed used car to work (no transit option),
and spends about $3,000 a month across ten categories.

## Finding
Jordan's household costs rose **25.0%** from 2019 to 2024, about two points more than
headline CPI (roughly 23%). That is about $750 more each month to buy the same things.

The gap with CPI comes from the car: car insurance rose about 45%, and the car payment
and gasoline about 27% each. Rent is the largest single driver of the increase (43% of it),
but it rose at about the same pace as everything else, so it does not explain the gap.

The result is most sensitive to the budget shares for phone and internet and for car
insurance. A five-point error in either share moves the headline by 1 to 1.5 points.

**Confidence: moderate.** Range: 24% to 26.5%, with 25% as the central figure.

## What the notebook does
1. **Basket:** builds a ten-category household basket and a weighted Laspeyres index (2019 = 100).
2. **Official series:** pulls six CPI series from FRED (headline, rent, food at home,
   used cars, gasoline, food away from home) and rebases them to 2019 = 100.
3. **Data audit:** checks coverage and missing months (October 2025 was not published
   because of the federal shutdown), compares full-history and common-sample
   (1981 to 2026) results, and compares seasonally adjusted with unadjusted used-car prices.
4. **AI expansion:** uses an AI-generated sensitivity analysis of the index to each
   category's weight, then checks it independently.

## How to run
Open the notebook in Google Colab and choose *Runtime › Restart and run all*.
It downloads data from FRED. To use the FRED API, add a key as a Colab Secret named
`FRED_API_KEY`. Without one, it uses FRED's public CSV links.
